# Canadian Responsible AI Grant Standard

## National Research, Funding, and Innovation Framework

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Document ID:** WIBC-CRAI-001  
**Status:** Foundational Standard  
**Maintained by:** Women in Blockchain Canada

## Purpose

The Canadian Responsible AI Grant Standard establishes a common framework for identifying, evaluating, developing, governing, and measuring research and innovation initiatives involving artificial intelligence, blockchain, Web4 infrastructure, cybersecurity, digital identity, autonomous agents, decentralized education, and public-interest technology.

The standard is designed to help researchers, universities, nonprofit organizations, startups, corporations, governments, and international partners build stronger funding strategies while maintaining responsible AI principles, transparent governance, measurable outcomes, and long-term public value.

## Scope

This standard applies to:

- AI research proposals
- multidisciplinary grant applications
- university-industry collaborations
- nonprofit innovation initiatives
- responsible AI pilots
- blockchain and Web4 projects
- digital governance programs
- AI safety and assurance research
- commercialization initiatives
- workforce and education programs
- knowledge infrastructure projects
- public-interest technology deployments

## Strategic Objective

The primary objective is to create a repeatable and evidence-based process for transforming research ideas into fundable, governable, and scalable innovation programs.

The standard connects five core activities:

1. Research
2. Partnerships
3. Funding
4. Implementation
5. Impact

These activities form the operating sequence for the broader AI Grant Strategy Series.

## Standard Lifecycle

Projects developed under this framework should move through the following stages:

### Stage 1 — Opportunity Identification

Identify the research problem, funding need, beneficiary group, policy relevance, and strategic alignment.

### Stage 2 — Evidence Development

Collect credible evidence, literature, stakeholder input, technical validation, and market or community data.

### Stage 3 — Partnership Formation

Build the institutional, academic, technical, governmental, nonprofit, and community partnerships required for delivery.

### Stage 4 — Funding Alignment

Match the project to suitable grants, challenge funds, procurement programs, philanthropic opportunities, and research partnerships.

### Stage 5 — Proposal Development

Prepare the theory of change, objectives, methodology, work plan, governance structure, risk framework, budget, and evaluation plan.

### Stage 6 — Responsible AI Review

Assess fairness, safety, privacy, transparency, explainability, human oversight, accessibility, accountability, and potential harms.

### Stage 7 — Implementation

Deliver the funded project through documented milestones, controls, stakeholder engagement, and evidence collection.

### Stage 8 — Evaluation

Measure outputs, outcomes, public value, research contribution, commercialization progress, and ecosystem effects.

### Stage 9 — Knowledge Publication

Publish appropriate research artifacts, datasets, standards mappings, implementation guides, and lessons learned.

### Stage 10 — Scale and Stewardship

Determine whether the project should be expanded, replicated, licensed, standardized, institutionalized, or maintained as a public good.

## Responsible AI Requirements

Projects should document how they address:

- human oversight
- accountability
- transparency
- explainability
- privacy
- cybersecurity
- data governance
- bias and fairness
- accessibility
- consent
- contestability
- auditability
- environmental impact
- misuse prevention
- lifecycle monitoring

## Evidence Hierarchy

The framework prioritizes evidence in the following order:

1. Primary laws, regulations, standards, and official program documents
2. Peer-reviewed research
3. Government and institutional reports
4. Verified implementation evidence
5. Structured stakeholder consultation
6. Reputable industry research
7. Expert judgment
8. LLM-assisted synthesis subject to human verification

AI-generated content must not be treated as independent evidence without validation against authoritative sources.

## Governance Principle

No funding opportunity should be evaluated solely on the amount available.

A high-quality grant strategy must consider:

- eligibility
- mission alignment
- readiness
- partnership burden
- compliance requirements
- reporting obligations
- delivery risk
- intellectual property implications
- data ownership
- public accountability
- post-funding sustainability

## Canadian Leadership Model

Canada serves as the initial implementation environment because of its established research institutions, public funding infrastructure, AI ecosystem, policy development capacity, multicultural population, and potential to connect responsible innovation with inclusive economic growth.

The framework is designed for later adaptation into a globally interoperable standard.

## Long-Term Outcome

The long-term goal is to establish a reusable Canadian standard for converting responsible AI research into funded, governed, measurable, and scalable innovation.

The standard should enable other regions, institutions, and research ecosystems to adapt the framework while preserving provenance, transparency, and public-interest safeguards.
