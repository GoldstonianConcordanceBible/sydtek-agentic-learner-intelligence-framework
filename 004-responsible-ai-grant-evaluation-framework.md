# Responsible AI Grant Evaluation Framework

## Standardized Evaluation Methodology for Responsible AI Research Funding

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Document ID:** WIBC-CRAI-004  
**Status:** Foundational Evaluation Framework  
**Maintained by:** Women in Blockchain Canada

---

# Purpose

This framework establishes a standardized methodology for evaluating responsible artificial intelligence grant opportunities, research proposals, innovation programs, and collaborative initiatives.

The framework enables transparent, evidence-based, and repeatable decision making across universities, nonprofit organizations, governments, industry, foundations, and international partnerships.

Rather than evaluating funding solely by award size, the framework measures strategic fit, research quality, implementation readiness, governance maturity, public value, and long-term sustainability.

---

# Evaluation Philosophy

Effective grant strategy begins with mission alignment.

Funding should support research that advances scientific knowledge, responsible innovation, societal benefit, workforce development, commercialization where appropriate, and long-term ecosystem growth.

Successful proposals balance innovation with accountability, measurable outcomes, and responsible stewardship of public resources.

---

# Evaluation Objectives

The framework is designed to:

- identify high-value funding opportunities;
- improve proposal quality;
- reduce unsuccessful applications;
- strengthen research partnerships;
- improve implementation readiness;
- support responsible AI governance;
- encourage interdisciplinary collaboration;
- increase long-term funding success;
- document evaluation decisions transparently.

---

# Evaluation Process

Projects are evaluated through eight sequential phases.

## Phase 1

Eligibility Review

Confirm organizational eligibility, applicant requirements, geographic restrictions, partnership requirements, funding limits, and submission criteria.

---

## Phase 2

Strategic Alignment

Determine alignment with:

- institutional mission;
- research priorities;
- responsible AI principles;
- funding objectives;
- public-interest goals;
- commercialization strategy.

---

## Phase 3

Research Assessment

Evaluate:

- novelty;
- scientific contribution;
- methodological quality;
- evidence base;
- feasibility;
- interdisciplinary integration.

---

## Phase 4

Responsible AI Assessment

Review:

- fairness;
- transparency;
- explainability;
- accountability;
- privacy;
- cybersecurity;
- accessibility;
- bias mitigation;
- human oversight;
- auditability.

---

## Phase 5

Implementation Review

Assess:

- project planning;
- governance;
- staffing;
- partnerships;
- timeline;
- milestones;
- procurement;
- budget realism.

---

## Phase 6

Impact Assessment

Evaluate expected impact on:

- research;
- society;
- economy;
- education;
- public policy;
- innovation;
- workforce development;
- commercialization.

---

## Phase 7

Risk Assessment

Review:

- technical risks;
- financial risks;
- governance risks;
- ethical risks;
- legal risks;
- operational risks;
- cybersecurity risks;
- sustainability risks.

---

## Phase 8

Funding Recommendation

Prepare the final recommendation supported by evidence, evaluation scores, reviewer observations, and documented rationale.

---

# Core Evaluation Domains

Each proposal should be evaluated across the following domains.

## Strategic Alignment

Questions include:

- Does the proposal align with funding priorities?
- Does it support institutional goals?
- Does it address a meaningful societal need?

---

## Scientific Merit

Evaluate:

- originality;
- literature foundation;
- methodology;
- technical rigor;
- expected contribution.

---

## Responsible AI

Determine whether the proposal demonstrates:

- trustworthy AI;
- human-centered design;
- transparency;
- governance;
- responsible deployment.

---

## Partnerships

Assess:

- quality of collaborators;
- complementary expertise;
- governance capacity;
- implementation capability;
- community engagement.

---

## Project Management

Review:

- milestones;
- work packages;
- timeline;
- resource allocation;
- project leadership.

---

## Budget

Evaluate:

- justification;
- efficiency;
- realism;
- sustainability;
- matching resources.

---

## Public Benefit

Assess potential contributions to:

- Canadian society;
- economic growth;
- education;
- healthcare;
- sustainability;
- digital inclusion;
- public trust.

---

## Knowledge Mobilization

Determine plans for:

- publications;
- open science;
- educational resources;
- software releases;
- datasets;
- policy guidance;
- standards development.

# Suggested Weighting Model

Organizations may customize weighting according to program objectives.

Example:

| Domain | Suggested Weight |
|---------|-----------------:|
| Strategic Alignment | 15% |
| Scientific Merit | 15% |
| Responsible AI | 15% |
| Partnerships | 10% |
| Implementation | 10% |
| Governance | 10% |
| Public Benefit | 10% |
| Commercialization | 5% |
| Budget | 5% |
| Sustainability | 5% |

Weights should total 100 percent.

---

# Five-Point Scoring Scale

| Score | Interpretation |
|--------|----------------|
| 1 | Very Weak |
| 2 | Limited |
| 3 | Adequate |
| 4 | Strong |
| 5 | Outstanding |

Each score should include written justification supported by documented evidence.

# Funding Priority Matrix

Projects may be classified as:

## Priority A

Highly recommended

Exceptional strategic alignment and strong implementation readiness.

---

## Priority B

Recommended

Strong proposal with moderate improvements identified.

---

## Priority C

Conditional

Fundable after revisions or additional partnerships.

---

## Priority D

Low Priority

Significant weaknesses require redevelopment.

---

## Priority E

Not Recommended

Does not satisfy essential program requirements.

# Responsible AI Review Checklist

Reviewers should confirm whether the proposal addresses:

- transparency;
- explainability;
- accountability;
- privacy;
- consent;
- accessibility;
- fairness;
- cybersecurity;
- environmental considerations;
- human oversight;
- auditability;
- lifecycle monitoring.

---

# Evidence Requirements

Evaluation should rely upon:

- official funding documents;
- peer-reviewed research;
- institutional policies;
- implementation evidence;
- verified datasets;
- documented partnerships;
- project plans;
- budget documentation.

---

# Reviewer Documentation

Each review should record:

- reviewer name or identifier;
- review date;
- evidence consulted;
- scoring rationale;
- identified strengths;
- identified weaknesses;
- recommendations;
- confidence level.

---

# Quality Assurance

Evaluation consistency should be improved through:

- standardized scoring guides;
- reviewer training;
- calibration exercises;
- periodic audits;
- documentation reviews;
- methodology updates.

# Long-Term Objective

The Responsible AI Grant Evaluation Framework establishes a transparent and repeatable evaluation process that enables Canadian organizations to prioritize funding opportunities, strengthen proposal quality, improve research outcomes, and contribute to a trustworthy and internationally adaptable responsible AI innovation ecosystem.
