# Purpose

The Responsible AI Safety and Constitutional Governance Framework establishes governance principles for the design, development, deployment, oversight, and continuous improvement of artificial intelligence systems.

The framework integrates responsible AI principles with constitutional governance concepts to support trustworthy, transparent, accountable, and human-centered AI across research, education, government, nonprofit organizations, and industry.

---

# Objectives

The framework seeks to:

- promote trustworthy AI;
- establish governance structures;
- strengthen accountability;
- support regulatory readiness;
- improve organizational oversight;
- encourage transparent decision-making;
- reduce AI-related risks;
- protect public interests;
- foster responsible innovation;
- enable sustainable AI adoption.

---

# Constitutional Governance Principles

AI governance should be guided by:

- Human dignity
- Human oversight
- Transparency
- Accountability
- Explainability
- Fairness
- Privacy
- Security
- Accessibility
- Sustainability
- Rule of law
- Public benefit

These principles should remain applicable regardless of changes in technology or implementation methods.

# Governance Structure

Organizations should establish:

- executive oversight;
- technical leadership;
- ethics review;
- risk management;
- compliance monitoring;
- security governance;
- stakeholder engagement;
- audit functions.

---

# Human Oversight

AI systems should support—not replace—appropriate human judgment.

Organizations should clearly define:

- decision authority;
- escalation procedures;
- override mechanisms;
- approval requirements;
- accountability responsibilities.

---

# Transparency

Organizations should document:

- system purpose;
- intended use;
- limitations;
- training data where appropriate;
- governance processes;
- evaluation methods;
- monitoring activities.

---

# Explainability

AI-supported decisions should be understandable to appropriate stakeholders.

Explainability should consider:

- technical users;
- decision makers;
- affected individuals;
- regulators;
- auditors.

---

# Accountability

Governance should clearly assign responsibility for:

- development;
- deployment;
- monitoring;
- maintenance;
- incident response;
- compliance;
- retirement.

Responsibility should remain with accountable human organizations.

---

# Privacy

Organizations should implement appropriate safeguards for:

- personal information;
- confidential data;
- research data;
- access control;
- retention;
- deletion;
- consent management.

---

# Fairness

Organizations should evaluate potential impacts on different populations and seek to reduce unjustified bias through:

- representative evaluation;
- documented testing;
- stakeholder engagement;
- ongoing monitoring.

---

# Security

Security programs should include:

- authentication;
- authorization;
- encryption;
- logging;
- monitoring;
- vulnerability management;
- incident response;
- disaster recovery.

---

# Accessibility

AI systems should promote equitable access through:

- inclusive design;
- usability testing;
- accessibility standards;
- multilingual support where appropriate;
- accommodation planning.

---

# Responsible AI Review Checklist

Organizations should evaluate:

- transparency;
- explainability;
- accountability;
- privacy;
- cybersecurity;
- fairness;
- accessibility;
- human oversight;
- governance maturity;
- regulatory readiness;
- environmental sustainability;
- public benefit.

---

# Governance Documentation

Projects should maintain:

- governance charter;
- risk register;
- decision log;
- audit records;
- incident reports;
- policy documents;
- approval history;
- review schedule;
- change log.

---

# Incident Management

Organizations should establish procedures for:

- identifying incidents;
- reporting concerns;
- investigating causes;
- implementing corrective actions;
- documenting lessons learned;
- communicating with stakeholders when appropriate.

---

# Compliance

Governance should align with applicable:

- laws;
- regulations;
- institutional policies;
- contractual obligations;
- funding requirements;
- recognized standards.

Organizations should periodically review governance as requirements evolve.

---

Governance effectiveness may be measured through:

- incident frequency;
- response times;
- stakeholder satisfaction;
- training completion;
- policy adherence;
- continuous improvement activities.

# Long-Term Objective

The Responsible AI Safety and Constitutional Governance Framework provides a durable governance foundation that enables Canadian organizations to design, deploy, and manage trustworthy AI systems while protecting public interests, supporting innovation, and maintaining transparent human accountability throughout the AI lifecycle.
