# Canadian Responsible AI Impact and Outcomes Dataset

## Canonical Metadata Standard for Measuring Research, Economic, Social, Institutional, Environmental, and Public-Interest Results

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Document ID:** WIBC-DS-018  
**Status:** Foundational Dataset Specification  
**Maintained by:** Women in Blockchain Canada

---

# Purpose

The Canadian Responsible AI Impact and Outcomes Dataset establishes a standardized, machine-readable framework for documenting the outputs, outcomes, impacts, benefits, harms, lessons, and long-term results of artificial intelligence, blockchain, Web4, cybersecurity, digital identity, data governance, and public-interest technology initiatives.

The dataset is designed to support grant reporting, program evaluation, policy analysis, institutional accountability, public transparency, commercialization assessment, knowledge mobilization, and longitudinal research.

It provides a structured method for distinguishing between activities completed, outputs produced, outcomes achieved, and broader impacts observed.

# Core Measurement Structure

The dataset should distinguish among:

1. Inputs
2. Activities
3. Outputs
4. Short-Term Outcomes
5. Intermediate Outcomes
6. Long-Term Outcomes
7. System-Level Impacts

These categories should not be used interchangeably.

---

# Attribution Levels

Impact claims should identify the strength of attribution.

## Level 1 — Association

The outcome occurred during the project period, but causation is not established.

## Level 2 — Contribution

The project likely contributed to the outcome alongside other factors.

## Level 3 — Strong Contribution

Evidence indicates that the project played a substantial role.

## Level 4 — Probable Attribution

Evaluation evidence strongly supports the project as a major cause.

## Level 5 — Demonstrated Attribution

A rigorous evaluation design supports a causal conclusion.

# Evidence Quality Levels

## Level 1 — Self-Reported

The result is reported by the project team without independent verification.

## Level 2 — Documented

Supporting records exist.

## Level 3 — Verified

The result has been reviewed against source evidence.

## Level 4 — Independently Evaluated

An independent reviewer has assessed the result.

## Level 5 — Replicated or Longitudinally Confirmed

The result has been reproduced, sustained, or confirmed over time.

# Limitations

The dataset recognizes that:

- impact may take years to appear;
- attribution is often difficult;
- baseline data may be incomplete;
- self-reported results may contain bias;
- financial proxies may be uncertain;
- negative outcomes may be underreported;
- indicators may not capture lived experience;
- institutional data may be inconsistent;
- comparisons across projects may be imperfect;
- public reporting may omit confidential information.

Impact claims should therefore be proportionate to the strength of available evidence.

---

# Long-Term Objective

The Canadian Responsible AI Impact and Outcomes Dataset establishes a transparent, version-controlled, machine-readable foundation for measuring whether responsible AI research, funding, governance, commercialization, education, and implementation create meaningful and sustainable value.

Its long-term objective is to connect public and private investment with verifiable outcomes, strengthen accountability, improve future grant strategy, and support evidence-based decisions across Canada’s responsible AI innovation ecosystem.
