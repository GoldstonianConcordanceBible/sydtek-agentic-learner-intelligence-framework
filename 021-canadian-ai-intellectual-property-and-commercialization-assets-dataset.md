# Canadian AI Intellectual Property and Commercialization Assets Dataset

## Canonical Metadata Standard for Patents, Copyrights, Trademarks, Trade Secrets, Licenses, Products, Ventures, and Technology-Transfer Assets

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Document ID:** WIBC-DS-021  
**Status:** Foundational Dataset Specification  
**Maintained by:** Women in Blockchain Canada

---

# Purpose

The Canadian AI Intellectual Property and Commercialization Assets Dataset establishes a standardized, machine-readable framework for documenting intellectual property, research outputs, licensing opportunities, products, ventures, commercialization pathways, technology-transfer activities, and market-ready assets across Canada’s responsible AI ecosystem.

The dataset is designed to support research commercialization, grant development, technology transfer, investor readiness, licensing, partnership formation, procurement, startup development, intellectual-property governance, and public-interest innovation.

It creates a structured connection between research activity and the assets capable of producing economic, institutional, social, or public benefit.

# Commercialization Readiness Levels

## Level 1 — Research Discovery

A potentially valuable research result or technical asset has been identified.

## Level 2 — Asset Definition

Ownership, inventorship, rights, and technical scope are documented.

## Level 3 — Protection Strategy

Intellectual-property protection and disclosure decisions have been established.

## Level 4 — Prototype Validation

The asset has been demonstrated through a prototype or controlled test.

## Level 5 — Market Validation

Potential users, customers, partners, or beneficiaries have provided evidence of demand.

## Level 6 — Commercialization Plan

A business, licensing, procurement, or deployment pathway has been documented.

## Level 7 — Transaction Ready

The asset has sufficient legal, technical, financial, and governance documentation for licensing, investment, procurement, or launch.

## Level 8 — Commercial Deployment

The asset is generating revenue, licensing income, procurement value, or sustained adoption.

## Level 9 — Scale and Portfolio Growth

The asset has expanded across markets, institutions, sectors, or jurisdictions.

---

# Technology Readiness Levels

## TRL 1 — Basic Principles Observed

Scientific or technical principles have been identified.

## TRL 2 — Technology Concept Formulated

Potential applications and technical concepts have been described.

## TRL 3 — Experimental Proof of Concept

Key functions have been demonstrated experimentally.

## TRL 4 — Component Validation

Components have been validated in a laboratory environment.

## TRL 5 — Validation in Relevant Environment

The technology has been tested under conditions resembling intended use.

## TRL 6 — Prototype Demonstration

A prototype has been demonstrated in a relevant environment.

## TRL 7 — System Demonstration

The system has been demonstrated in an operational environment.

## TRL 8 — System Complete

The system has been completed and qualified.

## TRL 9 — Operational Use

The technology has been proven through sustained operational use.

Ownership, inventorship, and rights are unclear.

## Level 2 — Documented

Assets, creators, owners, and basic rights are recorded.

## Level 3 — Protected

Protection, licensing, confidentiality, and disclosure processes are operational.

## Level 4 — Commercialization Aligned

Intellectual-property strategy is connected to product, market, grant, and partnership plans.

## Level 5 — Portfolio Governed

Assets are managed as a coordinated institutional or venture portfolio.

## Level 6 — Continuously Optimized

Licensing, market evidence, risk, valuation, impact, and portfolio decisions are regularly reviewed.

# Limitations

The dataset recognizes that:

- ownership may be disputed;
- legal rights vary by jurisdiction;
- patent applications may remain unpublished;
- patent grant does not establish market value;
- intellectual-property registration does not guarantee freedom to operate;
- commercialization claims may be overstated;
- private license terms may be unavailable;
- venture financial data may be confidential;
- product status may change quickly;
- valuation estimates are uncertain;
- legal interpretation requires qualified professional review;
- public records may contain delays or errors.

High-stakes legal, investment, licensing, or commercialization decisions should be verified through current primary records and qualified professional advice.

# Long-Term Objective

The Canadian AI Intellectual Property and Commercialization Assets Dataset establishes a transparent, version-controlled, machine-readable foundation for connecting Canadian research and technical innovation with ownership, licensing, product development, procurement, investment, entrepreneurship, and measurable public benefit.

Its long-term objective is to strengthen responsible commercialization, improve technology transfer, expand equitable participation in innovation, preserve creator and community rights, and increase the economic and societal value generated by Canada’s responsible AI ecosystem.
