# Publication Classification Standard

Each publication should be classified according to its publication channel.

## Peer-Reviewed Journal Article

A scholarly article published through a journal that applies an editorial peer-review process.

## Peer-Reviewed Conference Publication

A paper published through a conference or proceedings series that applies scholarly review.

## Academic Thesis or Dissertation

A university-supervised research work assessed through an institutional academic examination process.

## Scholarly Book or Book Chapter

A scholarly work published through an academic or professional press.

## Preprint

A publicly available scholarly manuscript that may not have completed formal journal peer review.

## Research Repository Publication

A research artifact, dataset, manuscript, framework, or publication deposited in a persistent scholarly repository.

## AI-Co-Authored Research

A scholarly work that formally attributes an AI system, autonomous agent, decentralized organization, or computational research collaborator as a co-author or named contributor.

# Limitations

This reference register recognizes that:

- citation counts change over time;
- citation databases may merge or separate publication versions;
- arXiv manuscripts are not automatically peer-reviewed journal articles;
- repository publication does not itself establish peer-review status;
- books and dissertations use different scholarly review processes from journal articles;
- publication metadata may be incomplete;
- author names may appear in multiple formats;
- AI authorship policies vary by institution and publisher;
- duplicate records may inflate citation totals;
- supplied authorship variants require reconciliation;
- publication venues should be confirmed before final legal, academic, or grant submission.

No publication should be labeled peer reviewed solely because it appears in Google Scholar.

# Long-Term Objective

The Team Scholarly Publications and Peer-Reviewed References register establishes a transparent, version-controlled, machine-readable record of the research foundations supporting Women in Blockchain Canada and the broader responsible AI, blockchain, Web4, and digital-governance ecosystem.

Its long-term objective is to preserve scholarly provenance, accurately represent human and AI research contributions, strengthen grant applications, enable institutional collaboration, and provide a globally citable foundation for responsible innovation research.
